# Fund Structure: Key CEO Insights

## Core Strategic Imperatives

**Priority Hierarchy**: Reliability > Regulatory Compliance > Profitability
- Automated markets are inherently fragile (tight coupling + complex interactions)
- Systemic failure is a certainty, not a possibility—structure must be resilient-first
- Internal reliability can paradoxically cause market instability if unchecked

**Critical Risk**: Even highly reliable firms can accelerate systemic crises through rapid, automated responses. Governance must manage the *rate* of response, not just correctness.

## Recommended Structure: Hybrid AI-Native Model

### Four-Layer Architecture

1. **Governance Layer** (Centralized Control)
   - Independent Risk Management (IRM): Real-time monitoring, override authority
   - Portfolio Allocation Committee (PAC): Dynamic, factor-based capital allocation
   - Risk & Trading Oversight Committee (RTOC): Strategic "kill switch" authority

2. **Infrastructure Layer** (Centralized Utility)
   - Technology/Data Services: Standardized platforms for all strategies
   - Execution Research Team: Dedicated team optimizing trade implementation across all pods

3. **Alpha Generation Layer** (Decentralized Innovation)
   - Strategy Development Teams (SDTs/Pods): Independent, specialized research teams
   - Each pod focuses on narrow mandates (specific asset class/factor/timeframe)
   - Organizational independence prevents groupthink, maximizes diverse alpha

4. **Interface Layer** (Human-AI Collaboration)
   - Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) at defined autonomy boundaries
   - Four critical roles: Corrective, Friction, Accountability, Justificatory

### Why Hybrid Beats Alternatives

- **Centralized**: Too slow for competitive alpha generation
- **Decentralized ("Pod Shop")**: Superior returns but coordination failure risk
- **Hybrid**: Balances speed (decentralized research) + control (centralized risk/infra)

## Operational Design Principles

### Function Separation (R-E-R)

1. **Research (Decentralized)**: SDTs develop alpha models independently
2. **Execution (Centralized)**: Shared execution team optimizes all trades—prevents reinventing infrastructure
3. **Risk (Centralized)**: IRM monitors all positions, manages correlations across strategies

### Handoff Protocols

- **Fast Path**: Routine automated checks (position limits, standard compliance)
- **Slow Path**: Human review for strategic breaches (volatility spikes, max drawdown approach)
- Trade-off: Accept increased latency for reliability when thresholds approached

### Coupling Strategy

**Tight Coupling Required**:
- Risk + Technology: Real-time monitoring
- Infrastructure: Standardized execution/data platforms

**Loose Coupling Required**:
- Strategy teams: Independent to foster innovation
- Investor flows: Decisions driven by fundamentals, not capital volatility

## Capital Allocation Authority

**Dynamic Over Static**: PAC reallocates capital based on:
- Factor exposure (not just asset class)
- Real-time correlation analysis
- Performance vs. risk budgets (max drawdown, beta neutrality)

**Non-Negotiable Power**: PAC can immediately reduce allocation to any strategy breaching risk parameters—decentralized profit pursuit remains subordinate to centralized reliability.

## AI Governance Framework

### Bounded Autonomy
- Define operational limits per algorithm (position size, data coverage)
- Automatic human escalation when boundaries breached
- Not error correction—deliberate design feature

### Human Roles in AI Systems
1. **Corrective**: Identify systemic errors, data drift, model bias
2. **Friction**: Slow automated decisions at risk thresholds
3. **Accountability**: Legal liability, deployment sign-off
4. **Justificatory**: Explain decisions for legitimacy/compliance

### Automation Bias Mitigation
- Clear corporate governance policies
- Human-centric system design
- Provide interpretable information, not black-box recommendations
- Oversight committees need technical expertise to challenge models

## Implementation Priorities

1. **Mandate Bounded Autonomy**: Program friction checkpoints into all algorithms; prioritize safety over speed
2. **Centralize Override Authority**: IRM/PAC must have non-negotiable power to halt strategies and reallocate capital
3. **Standardize Strategy Lifecycle**: Enforce common protocols (constraints, benchmarks, data) across all SDTs for scalability
4. **Implement Active HITL**: Design interfaces providing specialized, interpretable info to prevent automation bias
5. **Factor-Based Allocation**: PAC uses factor exposure (Value, Momentum, Quality) not asset classes for portfolio construction

## Scalability to 15+ Strategies

- Centralized infra allows all strategies to leverage optimized execution without rebuilding
- Standardized research protocols make strategies comparable/fungible for capital allocation
- Independent pods generate uncorrelated returns while shared infra ensures operational efficiency
- PAC internalizes correlations across all strategies—prevents hidden compounded exposure in crises

## Critical Success Factors

- **Technology**: All SDTs must use centralized platforms (no siloed infrastructure)
- **Risk Discipline**: IRM has real-time visibility + immediate override capability
- **Human Authority**: RTOC retains strategic kill switch despite automation advantages
- **Culture**: HRO mindset—preoccupation with failure, reluctance to oversimplify, sensitivity to operations
